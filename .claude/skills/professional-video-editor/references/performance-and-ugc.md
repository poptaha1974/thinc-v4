## Creative diagnosis

Separate platform metrics from business outcomes:

| Funnel stage | Signals |
|---|---|
| Stop | first-frame clarity, thumb-stop, first seconds |
| Hold | watch curve, hold rate, completion, rewatches |
| Believe | comments, saves, shares, proof engagement, landing behavior |
| Act | click, qualified lead, checkout, order |
| Deliver | confirmation, delivery, return, contribution margin |

Do not elect a creative from views or CTR alone when downstream evidence exists.

## Direct-response spine

Use the smallest structure that fits the awareness stage:

`hook -> problem/desire recognition -> mechanism/use -> result/proof -> objection reduction -> offer -> CTA`

The hook earns the next second; it does not need to explain the whole offer. Move the strongest credible proof early. Connect the CTA to the story's payoff.

## Hook and retention

Generate hooks from distinct mechanisms:

- visual anomaly or outcome;
- specific pain recognition;
- identity or aspiration;
- credible confession or repeated use;
- demonstration in progress;
- comparison with current alternative;
- timely offer or news;
- counter-intuitive information.

For each hook record: target viewer, open question, implied promise, shot/sound, proof required later, and disbelief or policy risk.

Avoid generic hooks detached from the footage. Do not use “wait until the end” unless the payoff justifies it.

## Proof and offer

Use a proof ladder, strongest available first:

1. controlled, continuous demonstration;
2. real use with visible mechanism and outcome;
3. repeated-use testimony with specific detail;
4. independent authority or documented source;
5. social proof with provenance;
6. product claim supported by packaging or approved evidence;
7. aesthetic result without controlled causality.

Label level 7 as visual aspiration, not causal proof.

Offer presentation hierarchy:

1. what the buyer receives;
2. main value or result;
3. price and payment/shipping conditions;
4. bonus or gift shown accurately;
5. risk reversal when real;
6. one CTA.

Keep end cards brief enough to read, then maintain motion or evidence rather than repeating a static card.

## UGC language

Preserve the creator's specificity, texture, and natural timing. Prefer first-person experience over institutional copy. Keep imperfections that signal humanity; remove only those that obstruct meaning.

Recommended coverage:

- face-to-camera hook;
- product in hand;
- opening, texture, dosage, or setup;
- real application;
- objection or caveat;
- result in consistent light;
- tactile action such as brushing, pouring, clicking, wiping, or wearing;
- direct CTA.

Never manufacture a before shot from unrelated footage. When no before exists, build proof from application, continuity, mechanism, and precise testimony.

## Testing system

Test in layers: angle/motivation -> creative concept/proof -> hook -> body pacing/format -> offer -> CTA/end card -> audience/placement -> durability at higher spend.

Hold other major variables stable. Predeclare the metric and threshold appropriate to the stage. A changed font, caption color, or song is not a new angle.

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | Why should this variant perform differently? |
| Constant | What remains unchanged? |
| Variable | One major change being tested |
| Leading metric | Early signal |
| Business metric | Downstream quality/economics |
| Decision | keep, iterate, pause, or reject rule |
